Measured whether five well-known equity factor premia (SMB, HML, momentum, RMW, CMA)
decay after the paper that documented each one, splitting each factor's monthly returns
into in-sample and out-of-sample Sharpe ratios at its publication year. All five factors
decayed out-of-sample (e.g. SMB annualized Sharpe 0.25 in-sample to 0.05 out-of-sample,
CMA 0.56 to 0.04), and all five post-publication Sharpes are statistically
indistinguishable from a noise floor built from randomly-signed synthetic factors of
matched volatility with no true edge. The market factor and an equal-weight Fama-French
3-factor tilt, run through the identical pre/post split as a placebo, went the other way,
higher out-of-sample in every case, so the decay is not just "any two multi-decade
windows differ." The genuinely surprising part: a literal reading of "decay ratio outside
the noise floor" finds 0 of 5 factors clear that bar, the exact opposite of the headline,
because a ratio of two Sharpes has a noise floor so wide from occasional near-zero
denominators that almost nothing ever falls outside it; comparing the out-of-sample
Sharpe itself against a noise floor of out-of-sample Sharpes is what actually tests the
claim, and that is where the 5 of 5 result comes from.
